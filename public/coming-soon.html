Work in progress · not released

This page collects active development tracks that are not part of the current VKF release. Scope, performance and visuals may change. It is a view into verified work, not a release-date promise.

0.5.0 — bootstrapped, faster compiler

VKF 0.5.0 is the active compiler-development track. The compiler is moving toward a complete staged bootstrap while the existing implementation remains the semantic oracle. Each added language slice must preserve exact behavior before it becomes part of the bootstrap path.

The same track develops function- and scope-specific optimization policies. Proven policy results can be reused while function structure, dependencies and argument types remain unchanged. The goal is faster generated programs without turning every incremental build into a full policy search.

No 0.5.0 compiler package is available yet. Use the Download page for the current 0.4.5 release.

0.6.0 — pre-generation visuals and physics

The 0.6.0 pre-generation (pre-gen) track explores shared simulation and visualization systems before their public API is fixed. Media below is test-generated proof from verified development runs, not final product footage.

Only the latest image and video are retained for each category. A newer proof replaces the previous one to keep the public site small.

Water and shared physics

The previous water video has been withdrawn because its splash breakup and low-resolution warm caustic did not meet the verification bar. A replacement capture is in progress. It uses the shared 3D rigid-body and APIC liquid state, gravity at -9.82 m/s², two low rebounds, a chamber-wide connected water surface and directional ejecta derived from the measured impact field. No new water media will be linked here until the complete GPU capture has passed visual review.

Generated stones

This proof uses five current generated stone meshes and the shared rigid-contact model. Every retained contact is checked against the triangles; intersecting placements do not pass the proof.

Tree generation

The current tree proof contains 252 recursively divided branches, 1,126 twigs and 5,180 retained leaves. A common bark field covers the complete woody network with high-frequency spatial variation. Roughness changes continuously with radius, so thinner branches become smoother without a material jump at a fork. Leaf contact bent 2,281 leaves and rejected 304 unresolved candidates; the retained result has zero measured penetration.

Stanford Bunny and planar mirrors

This 12-second native WebGPU capture is the current red-and-green-light Stanford Bunny scene rendered by the same developing 3D pipeline. A planar mirror reflects the camera across its plane, uses the mirror rectangle as an off-axis viewing aperture, clips geometry behind the plane, renders the reflected scene into a texture, then samples that texture on the mirror surface. The bunny and lights are not duplicated scene objects. Curved mirrors require a different mapping and are not claimed by this proof.

The Stanford Bunny mesh comes from the Stanford Computer Graphics Laboratory and is used here for non-commercial research and educational development.

Sand

Sand is an active 0.6.0 category. Its public image and video will appear when the current dense-grain surface proof meets the verification bar. Older coarse or reflective prototypes are not presented as current work.
